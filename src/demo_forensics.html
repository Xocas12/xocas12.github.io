Integer-percentage turnout test

Kobak, Shpilkin and Pshenichnikov (2016) found that Russian precincts, in elections up to 2012, reported whole-number percentages, such as a turnout of 85.0% or 95.0%, more often than chance allows, and read it as officials writing down a target instead of a count. The same test, run with forensic-elections on 93,601 precincts of the 2018 presidential election, finds 11,907 on a whole percent where chance predicts 10,818 ± 90: 1,089 too many, a z-score of 12.2, mostly at turnouts of 80% and above. The highlighted bars are whole percentages; the line is what chance predicts. This is a pattern, not a verdict on any precinct, and the false-positive control, which should find nothing in Poland 2010 and Spain 2011, has not been run (what the results do not claim).
